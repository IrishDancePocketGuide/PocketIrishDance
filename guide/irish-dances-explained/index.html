The Irish dances, explained

Irish dancers learn soft shoe dances first (reel, light jig, slip jig, single jig), then hard shoe dances (treble jig, hornpipe, treble reel). Set dances and team dances come later.

Soft shoe dances

Reel

A fast soft shoe dance in 2/4 or 4/4 time. Usually the first competition dance.

Light jig

A quick soft shoe dance in 6/8. Often danced at beginner levels.

Slip jig

A graceful soft shoe dance in 9/8. Mostly danced by girls.

Single jig (hop jig)

A soft shoe jig in 6/8 or 12/8. Whether it's offered varies by region.

Hard shoe dances

Treble jig (heavy jig)

Say “TREB-ul”. A slow, heavy shoe jig. A core hard shoe dance.

Hornpipe

A heavy shoe dance in 2/4 or 4/4. A core hard shoe dance.

Treble reel

A hard shoe dance to fast reel music, packed with rhythm. Popular in shows. At a feis it's usually a special or fun competition, not a main dance.

Traditional speed (fast jig, fast hornpipe)

A treble jig or hornpipe danced at a quicker speed, used by newer dancers. Your dancer's level decides which speed they dance, so check the syllabus.

Set dances

Set dance

A hard shoe dance to a set tune. There are traditional sets and own choice sets. Appears from the middle levels upward.

Traditional set (trad set)

A set dance with set steps that every school learns, like St. Patrick's Day or The Blackbird. Danced at the lower and middle grades.

Own choice set (open set, contemporary set, non-traditional set)

A set dance where the dancer picks the tune and dances their teacher's own steps. This is the set danced in championship rounds, not a traditional set.

Team dances

Ceili

Say “KAY-lee”. A group dance, or a social dance night. Team dances at feiseanna and championships.

Figure dance (team dance)

A choreographed group dance, often 4 to 16 dancers. Team practice adds to the schedule.

Two-hand

A team dance for a pair of dancers. Often a dancer's first team competition.

Eight-hand (four-hand, six-hand)

A céilí team named for its size: 4, 6 or 8 dancers, dancing a traditional céilí dance. Teams don't have to be evenly split between boys and girls.

The look

Carriage

How the upper body is held: straight back, still shoulders, arms close to the sides. Good carriage makes the footwork stand out.

Turnout

Feet and legs turned outward from the hips, so the toes point out to the sides. Judges look closely at it; it's one of the first things teachers correct.

Steps

Short choreographed sections of a dance, each about 8 bars a side. Teachers give new steps as dancers move up.
