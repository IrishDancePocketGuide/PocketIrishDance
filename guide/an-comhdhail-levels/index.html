An Comhdháil Irish dance levels, explained

The An Comhdháil level ladder from Beginners A to Championship: what each level dances, how a dancer moves up, and what it means for parents.

  • Beginners A
  • Beginners B
  • Beginners C
  • Pre-Open
  • Open
  • Championship

1Beginners A

Bun Grád

What they dance
Basic steps in reel, light jig, slip jig, single jig, hornpipe, treble jig and traditional sets.
Moving up
Win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers, then move up in that dance.
For parents
Class costume only; no solo costumes in Beginners A, B or C.

2Beginners B

Bun Grád

What they dance
Slightly harder versions of the basic steps.
Moving up
Win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers.
For parents
Beginner figure (team) dances are open to Beginners A and B.

3Beginners C

Bun Grád

What they dance
A further step up from Beginners B.
Moving up
Win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers.
For parents
Beginner cup competitions let a dancer move a dance up by winning the cup.

4Pre-Open

Meán Grád

What they dance
Solo dances in a set age group; a dancer may dance one year above their age.
Moving up
Win 1st place in a dance with at least 5 dancers.
For parents
Win in an older age group and you can come back to it the next year.

5Open

Ard Grád

What they dance
Open to everyone, in your own age group.
Moving up
The top grade for solo dances. Dancing an Open solo is needed before entering a Championship.
For parents
A dancer can be Open in some dances and lower in others.

6Championship

What they dance
A light and a heavy dance, judged by 1 or 3 adjudicators (5 at the World Championships). Open Championship starts at Under 10.
Moving up
Win with 1 judge: Open in those dances for the year. Win with 3 judges: Open in every dance for life.
For parents
Provincial Championships are the route to the World Championships.
