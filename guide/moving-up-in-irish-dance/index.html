How moving up works in Irish dance

At the lower levels, dancers usually move up one dance at a time, by placing high enough in that dance. So a dancer can be at one level in reel and another in light jig. The placing needed, and how many dancers must be in the competition, depends on your organization.

CLRG

These are the CLRG level names used at feiseanna in North America (the United States, Canada and Mexico).

Beginner 1

Place 1st, 2nd or 3rd in a dance, then move up in that dance.

Beginner 2

Place 1st, 2nd or 3rd in a dance.

Novice

Place 1st or 2nd in a dance.

Open Prizewinner

Win 1st in a dance.

Preliminary Championship

Win twice, or meet your region's rules. In the US Southern Region it takes 3 wins to move up to Open.

Open Championship

The top level.

An Comhdháil

Beginners A

Win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers, then move up in that dance.

Beginners B

Win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers.

Beginners C

Win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers.

Pre-Open

Win 1st place in a dance with at least 5 dancers.

Open

The top grade for solo dances. Dancing an Open solo is needed before entering a Championship.

Championship

Win with 1 judge: Open in those dances for the year. Win with 3 judges: Open in every dance for life.

WIDA

Beginner

Win 1st place in a dance with 5 or more dancers, then move up in that dance.

Primary

Win 1st place in a dance with 5 or more dancers.

Intermediate

Win 1st place in a dance with 5 or more dancers. After winning 4 Intermediate dances (2 of them hard shoe), move on to Preliminary Championship.

Open

The top solo grade.

Preliminary Championship

Win 3 Preliminary Championships with at least 5 dancers (at least 3 in America and Australasia)

Open Championship

The top level. Winning one moves all of a dancer's solo dances up to Open.

Everything about WIDA levels

CRDM

Beginner A

Win 1st place in a dance with 6 or more dancers, then move up in that dance. With fewer than 6, a dancer can win that dance 3 times before moving up.

Beginner B

Win 1st place in a dance with 6 or more dancers, or win it 3 times with fewer.

Intermediate

Win 1st place in a dance with 6 or more dancers, or win it 3 times with fewer.

Open

The top solo grade.

Preliminary Championship

Winning moves those 2 dances to Open. After 3 wins a dancer can't enter it again.

Open Championship

The top level. Winning one moves all of a dancer's dances to Open.

Quick answers

How do dancers move up in CLRG?

Beginner 1: place 1st, 2nd or 3rd in a dance, then move up in that dance. Beginner 2: place 1st, 2nd or 3rd in a dance. Novice: place 1st or 2nd in a dance. Open Prizewinner: win 1st in a dance.

How do dancers move up in An Comhdháil?

Beginners A: win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers, then move up in that dance. Beginners B: win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers. Beginners C: win 1st place in a dance with at least 10 dancers. Pre-Open: win 1st place in a dance with at least 5 dancers.

How do dancers move up in WIDA?

Beginner: win 1st place in a dance with 5 or more dancers, then move up in that dance. Primary: win 1st place in a dance with 5 or more dancers. Intermediate: win 1st place in a dance with 5 or more dancers. After winning 4 Intermediate dances (2 of them hard shoe), move on to Preliminary Championship.

How do dancers move up in CRDM?

Beginner A: win 1st place in a dance with 6 or more dancers, then move up in that dance. With fewer than 6, a dancer can win that dance 3 times before moving up. Beginner B: win 1st place in a dance with 6 or more dancers, or win it 3 times with fewer. Intermediate: win 1st place in a dance with 6 or more dancers, or win it 3 times with fewer.
