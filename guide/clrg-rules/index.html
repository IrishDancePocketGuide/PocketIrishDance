CLRG rules in plain words

Short answers to the questions parents ask most about An Coimisiún le Rincí Gaelacha (CLRG) rules. Taken from the CLRG Official Rulebook (V6, September 2026).

  • At the feis
  • Steps and technique

Entering competitions

Who registers my dancer for a CLRG feis?

For a regular feis, you do: families register their own dancer, usually online. It's wise to check with your teacher (TCRG) first about which feis and which dances. For majors such as the Oireachtas, Nationals and Worlds, the teacher registers the dancer, not the family. Either way, your dancer must be taught by a CLRG-registered teacher (a TCRG for solos, a TCRG or TMRF for teams).

Rulebook section 1.1

Can my dancer also compete in other organizations' competitions?

No. Dancers of a CLRG teacher may not compete in events that aren't run under CLRG, apart from a few CLRG recognises (such as Scór) and approved "open platform" events. Check with your teacher before entering anything else.

Rulebook section 1.1, 3.1

At the feis

Can I film or photograph my dancer?

Only in the first two grades (Beginner and Advanced Beginner, called Bun Grád and Tús Grád in the rulebook), and only if local law and your region allow it. In every other grade, photos and video of dancers dancing are forbidden.

Rulebook section 2.7

What is the "arms up" check side stage?

Before each round, dancers lining up side stage must be able to raise their arms to shoulder height. A dancer whose costume stops this isn't allowed to dance. Arm or posture aids ("carriage aids") are banned, apart from medical ones.

Rulebook section 5.4

Steps and technique

When can my dancer dance on their toes (en pointe)?

Not at all up to and including the Under 12 age group. Dancers moving into Under 13 in January may start from 1 September the year before.

Rulebook section 4.2

Shoes and costume

What can my dancer wear at Beginner and Advanced Beginner?

Up to and including Under 12, only a school class costume, or a simple outfit: blouse or shirt with a skirt or trousers, a polo top with a skirt or trousers, or a bodysuit with a skirt. No low-cut tops or short tight skirts.

Rulebook section 5.1.8

When can my dancer wear make-up?

No make-up for anyone in the Under 10 age group or younger, in solos or teams; that includes false eyelashes and tan on the face. No make-up in the first two grades up to and including Under 12. It goes by the competition's age group, not the dancer's own age.

Rulebook section 5.2

What are the rules for dresses?

Necklines at the collarbone or higher, a full front, sides and back, and sleeves. Underwear must cover the midriff. Skirts over bodysuits should reach mid-thigh at the back. No feathers and no cartoon characters. If a dress is too short, judges can ask for thick black tights.

Rulebook section 5.1.3–5.1.13

Can a championship dress show names or past wins?

No. In championships judged by 3 or more adjudicators, solo costumes and headwear can't have names, initials, or designs that show past achievements (such as globes).

Rulebook section 5.1.1–5.1.2

What are the rules for hard shoes?

Heels can be at most 1.5 inches high and made of leather, plastic or fiberglass, with no metal except the nails or screws holding them on. Altering shoes, wearing them on the wrong feet, or adding heels and tips to soft shoes can get a dancer disqualified.

Rulebook section 5.3

Changing schools

What happens if we change schools?

Your dancer has a 3-month "restyling" break from competing, whether they come from a CLRG school or another organization. It starts on the date the transfer notice is posted or emailed. Competing during it can add up to 3 more months.

Rulebook section 2.1

Are there exceptions to the 3-month restyling?

Yes. It doesn't apply to a beginner who spent 3 months or less at their first school, or to a dancer who hasn't been to any Irish dance class for at least a year. For anything else you can appeal using the form on clrg.ie.

Rulebook section 2.1.3, 2.1.5

Grade exams

What are grade exams?

Optional CLRG exams in dance skills, from Grade 1 up to Grade 10, separate from competitions. Your teacher has to request them.

Rulebook section 6.3
