The Oireachtas, explained

The Oireachtas (say “oh-ROCK-tus”), also called Regionals, is the regional championship, held once a year. Qualifying dancers compete for places at Nationals and Worlds.

The words around it

RegionCLRG only

A geographic area under CLRG with its own regional body and Oireachtas. Your region decides your Oireachtas and some local rules.

Majors (major championships)

The big championships above a local feis, such as the Oireachtas, Nationals, the All-Irelands and Worlds. They run over several days and usually need a qualifying result. The teacher usually enters dancers for these, and travel is often involved.

Recall

Being called back after the first rounds to dance the final round. A recall is itself an achievement worth celebrating.

World qualifier (WQ)CLRG only

A dancer who has earned a place at Worlds. Most qualify at their Oireachtas, and some at Nationals or the All-Irelands. Qualifying places are limited, so it's a big moment.

National qualifier (NQ)CLRG only

A dancer who has earned a place at Nationals in their solo competition, usually through an Oireachtas result. In North America, check the deadlines with your teacher.

Nationals

National championship (for example North American Nationals or the Great Britain Championships) Usually needs a qualifying result for championship dancers.

NAIDC (North American Nationals, NANs)CLRG only

The North American Irish Dance Championships, held each summer and hosted in turn by the North American regions. A second route to qualifying for Worlds, after the Oireachtas.

Worlds (Oireachtas Rince na Cruinne)CLRG only

Say “oh-ROCK-tus RIN-kuh nuh KRIN-yuh”. The CLRG World Championships. Qualification only, usually through a regional or national event.

All-Irelands

The All-Ireland Championships, held in Ireland. A major championship open to dancers from abroad.

Getting ready

Months before

  • Confirm your dancer is eligible and how entries are made
    In CLRG, the teacher (TCRG) registers dancers for majors like the Oireachtas, Nationals and Worlds, not the parent. Make sure they have everything they need from you in time.
  • Book the hotel as soon as the venue is announced
  • Ask for time off work and school if needed
  • Learn how recalls and qualifying places work for this event
  • Budget for entry, travel, hotel, food, extra lessons and costume care

Weeks before

  • Find your dancer's day and approximate time
  • Get the dress cleaned, crystals checked and any repairs done
  • Book wig styling or tan appointments if you use them
  • Make sure shoes are broken in, not brand new
  • Keep up extra classes or workshops the school offers

At the event

  • Pack enough food and drink for a long day
  • Plan rest between rounds: feet up, quiet, away from the crowd
  • Know where and when recalls are posted
  • Save the results link so you can follow the rounds

Whatever happens

  • Tell your dancer you're proud of the work they put in
  • Let the teacher explain results; ask privately if you have questions
  • Talk about goals for next season after a few days off
