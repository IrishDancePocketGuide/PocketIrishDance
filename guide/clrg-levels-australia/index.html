CLRG Irish dance levels in Australia, explained

CLRG in Australia, run by the Australian Irish Dancing Association (AIDA). Australia has five grades: Beginner, Primary, Elementary, Intermediate and Open. Elementary has no North American match. Some feiseanna also have a Novice grade below Beginner for first-timers.

A “Novice” in Australia is below Beginner. In North America, Novice is the third level. Same word, different level.

1Beginner

Bun-Grád on AIDA syllabi. Like Beginner 1 in North America. Most dancers start here

What they dance
Reel, light jig, often single jig and slip jig.
Moving up
Win 1st in a dance, or your teacher can move your dancer up. Check with your teacher how many dancers a win needs.
For parents
School costume or a simple outfit, soft shoes only.

2Primary

Tús-Grád on AIDA syllabi. Like Beginner 2 (Advanced Beginner) in North America

What they dance
Soft shoe dances plus treble jig and hornpipe.
Moving up
Win 1st in a dance, or your teacher can move your dancer up. Next comes Elementary.
For parents
Hard shoes are usually bought around here.

3Elementary

Mean-Grád (1) on AIDA feis syllabi

What they dance
The same dances and steps as Intermediate, but shorter: dancers do two steps of their heavy jig or hornpipe instead of three.
Moving up
Win 1st in a dance, then move up to Intermediate in that dance. AIDA's grade rules aren't published online, so check with your teacher how many dancers must be in the competition.
For parents
Hard shoe dances get more advanced here.

4Intermediate

Mean-Grád on AIDA syllabi. Like Novice in North America

What they dance
All the main dances, plus a traditional set at some feiseanna.
Moving up
Win 1st in a dance, then move up to Open in that dance.
For parents
Intermediate dancers can compete at State and National Championships, and can choose to enter the Preliminary Championship at the State Championships.

5Open

Ard-Grád on AIDA syllabi. Like Open Prizewinner in North America

What they dance
Same dances, with more advanced steps.
Moving up
The top grade.
For parents
Often the last stage before championships.

6Preliminary Championship

What they dance
One competition of two rounds: soft shoe, then hard shoe. A panel of judges watches both rounds. For each round one judge is picked at random, and that judge's placings count as everyone's round placing. The overall place comes from all the judges' scores added up, so it can differ from the round placings.
Moving up
Rules are set by AIDA. Ask your teacher.
For parents
Recalls and Oireachtas qualification come into play.

7Open Championship

What they dance
Like Preliminary Championship but with three rounds: soft shoe, hard shoe, and a set round (own choice set). Each round is placed by one judge picked at random; the overall place comes from all the judges' scores added up. Judged by a panel (3, or 5 at majors). The path to Nationals, All-Irelands and Worlds.
Moving up
The top level.
For parents
The highest costs and the most travel.

AAdult Championship

For dancers 18 and over; also called Adult Champ

What they dance
One competition, usually three rounds: hard shoe, soft shoe, and a traditional set. Like other championships, a panel of judges decides one overall place. Who can enter: dancers who started Irish dance at 18 or older, or came back after 5 or more years away, and haven't danced at a high youth level recently (in the Southern Region, no top 3 in Preliminary Championship in the last 10 years and no Open Championship in the last 15).
Moving up
Usually your teacher decides when to move up to Advanced Adult Championship. Rules differ by region.
For parents
Adults only dance adult competitions. Adult Champ is not a step towards youth Open Championship.

AAdvanced Adult Championship

Also called Advanced Adult Champ; some feiseanna split it into Advanced Adult Prelim and Advanced Adult Open

What they dance
One competition, usually three rounds: hard shoe, soft shoe, and a set dance (own choice set). Judged by a panel, with one overall place. For adults who placed top 3 in Preliminary Championship, or danced Open Championship, as a youth dancer (in the Southern Region, within the last 10 and 15 years).
Moving up
The top adult level. Once an adult dances Advanced Adult, they can't go back to youth competitions.
For parents
Recalls work like other championships: at the Southern Region Oireachtas, only recalled dancers dance the set round.

AIDA's own grade rules (how many dancers must be in a competition to move up) aren't published online, so the numbers aren't shown here. Your teacher has them.

Moving-up rules are set by each regional council and can change. Your teacher and the feis syllabus have the final word.
