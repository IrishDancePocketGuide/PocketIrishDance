Your dancer's first feis

Read the syllabus, register before the deadline, and arrive early. Stages can run early, and parking and check-in take longer than you'd think. Get your dancer to side stage when the competition before theirs starts, then cheer, and start the ride home with “I loved watching you dance”.

Before the day

Choose and enter

  • Check with the teacher that your dancer is ready and which feis to go to
  • Read the syllabus
    It lists the competitions by number, the age groups, the fees, the entry deadline and any special rules.
  • Work out your dancer's competition age
    Most feiseanna use the dancer's age on January 1 of the year, not their age on the day.
  • Enter only the dances and level the teacher approved
  • Register before the deadline
    For a regular feis you register your dancer yourself, usually online, after checking with the teacher. Late entries often cost extra, and some feiseanna cap entries or close early.
  • Save the confirmation email and any competitor number

Plan the day

  • Look up the venue, parking and drive time
  • Watch for the stage schedule, often posted a few days before
    Screenshot it. Wi-Fi at venues can be poor.
  • Book a hotel early if it's far away
  • Confirm what to wear with the school: class costume, skirt and top, or school outfit
  • Make a plan for siblings and who's driving

The week before

  • Try on the whole outfit, shoes included, in case your dancer has grown
  • Clean and check shoes: laces, straps and buckles
  • Do a practice run of the competition hair or wig
    Wig and bun pins take practice. A trial run saves a stressful morning.
  • Ask the teacher whether to practice right up to the day or ease off
  • Protect sleep the last two nights

On the day

Morning

  • A good breakfast before the costume goes on
  • Hair, wig and any makeup done before leaving, or allow extra time at the venue
  • Arrive at least 45 minutes before your first competition
    Stages can run early, and parking and check-in take longer than you'd think.

At the venue

  • Check in at the registration desk and collect the competitor number
  • Pin the number on the front where the judge can see it
  • Find your dancer's stage and check which competition it's on now
  • Set up a base near the school's group, without blocking walkways
  • Let the teacher know you've arrived
  • Warm up: gentle moving, skips and pointing feet, not long stretches on a cold floor

Before each dance

  • Get to side stage when the competition before yours starts
    If your dancer misses their turn, the competition usually won't wait.
  • Quick check: shoes tied and taped, socks up, number straight, wig secure
  • Keep it short and positive. One focus point, then a hug
  • Watch, cheer, and don't film unless the feis allows it

After dancing

  • Check the next competition time and stage
  • Cover up the costume to eat and drink
  • Find out where results are posted or announced
    Results may go up on a board, be read out at awards, or appear online. Some feiseanna also give out comment sheets.
  • Photograph the results and any comment sheets

The ride home

  • Start with 'I loved watching you dance'
  • Let your dancer talk first; save feedback for another day
  • Don't pick apart the judging
    Each judge sees things a little differently. If you have questions, ask the teacher privately later.
  • Share the results and comment sheets with the teacher
  • Unpack, air out shoes and hang up the costume that night

Feis manners

Around the hall

  • Don't save rows of seats or spread bags across chairs
  • Keep doorways, stairs and side stage clear
  • Never walk in front of a stage while a dancer is on it
  • Keep voices down near the stages
  • Follow the feis's photo and video rules
    Most competitions ban filming dancers on stage to protect children and choreography.
  • Clean up your spot before you leave

Being a good sport

  • Clap for every dancer, not just your school's
  • Celebrate wins warmly but not loudly; the child beside you may be heartbroken
  • Never criticize judges, dancers or other schools, including online
  • Never approach a judge about a result
    Questions about marks go to your teacher. Organizations have their own process for formal concerns.
  • Thank the volunteers, musicians and stage helpers
  • Help the family who looks lost; you were new once too

Quick answers

How early should we arrive at a feis?

Arrive at least 45 minutes before your first competition. Stages can run early, and parking and check-in take longer than you'd think.

Can I film my dancer at a feis?

Most competitions ban filming dancers on stage to protect children and choreography.

What if my dancer misses their turn?

If your dancer misses their turn, the competition usually won't wait.
