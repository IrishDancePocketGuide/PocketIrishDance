Irish dance shoes, explained

Irish dancers wear two kinds of shoes. Soft shoes are ghillies for girls and reel shoes for boys. Hard shoes have fiberglass tips and heels that make the rhythm. Have the teacher check the fit. They should be snug, like a second skin, and will stretch.

The shoes

Soft shoe

Light, lace-up dances: reel, light jig, slip jig, single jig. The first dances most beginners learn.

Ghillies (pumps, pomps)

Say “GILL-eez”. Soft, laced shoes worn by girls and women. Need replacing as feet grow; check the fit with the teacher.

Reel shoes

Soft shoes for boys, like a jazz shoe with a heel that clicks. The boys' equivalent of ghillies.

Hard shoe (heavy shoe, jig shoe)

Dances in shoes with fiberglass tips and heels that make rhythm: treble jig, hornpipe, treble reel. A second pair of shoes, usually bought a bit later.

Buckles

Decorations, often sparkly, on the front strap of hard shoes. Small and easy to lose, so pack a spare.

Elastics (shoe straps, white tape)

Bands or straps that go over the shoes to keep them on while dancing. Pack spares, because a shoe coming off can cost a place.

Socks

Poodle socks

Long white ribbed socks. Held up with sock glue.

Sock glue

Roll-on adhesive that keeps socks in place. Bring it to every feis.

Shoe tips from other parents

  • Write your dancer's name inside both shoes
  • Check shoe size every few months while your dancer is growing
  • Spare laces, plus a spare buckle or strap for hard shoes
  • A black marker to touch up scuffs on hard shoes

Quick answers

When do Irish dancers get hard shoes?

Usually once the teacher says so, often around CLRG's Beginner 2 level. Lots of families buy too early. Second-hand is very common for all of these.

How should ghillies fit?

Have the teacher check the fit. They should be snug, like a second skin, and will stretch.
