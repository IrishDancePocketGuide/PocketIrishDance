How much does Irish dance cost?

It depends a lot on your school, your country and how far your dancer goes. Costs start small, with class fees and soft shoes, and grow as dancers move up: hard shoes, feis entry fees, then a solo dress, a wig and travel to major championships. Ask your teacher for the school's yearly costs before you start.

When your dancer starts

Ask about class fees, registration, shoes, school costume rental and feis entry fees so there are no surprises.

Buy (only what you need)

  • Soft shoes (ghillies for girls, reel shoes for boys)
    Have the teacher check the fit. They should be snug, like a second skin, and will stretch.
  • White socks for class (poodle socks come later for feiseanna)
  • Class wear the school asks for, often shorts or leggings and a school t-shirt
  • A named water bottle and a bag that's just for dance
  • Hold off on hard shoes, wigs and dresses until the teacher says
    Lots of families buy too early. Second-hand is very common for all of these.

Feis entries

Syllabus

The list of competitions, ages and fees at a feis. Read it before you register.

Specials (special competitions, trophy dance)

Extra competitions at a feis that don't count toward moving up, such as a trophy dance, a traditional set or a treble reel. A fun way to dance more, but they add to your entry fees.

For a regular feis you register your dancer yourself, usually online, after checking with the teacher. Late entries often cost extra, and some feiseanna cap entries or close early.

As your dancer moves up

Hard shoe (heavy shoe, jig shoe)

Dances in shoes with fiberglass tips and heels that make rhythm: treble jig, hornpipe, treble reel. A second pair of shoes, usually bought a bit later.

Solo dress

A dancer's own competition dress. One of the biggest costs; rules govern when it's allowed.

Wig

A curled wig worn in competition, usually by girls. Often from the middle levels; check age and level rules.

Tan

Spray or bottled tan for legs. Rules on makeup and tan vary by age and level.

Before you buy

  • Check the rules for when a solo dress, wig and makeup are allowed
    It depends on your organization, region and level. Some beginner levels don't allow them at all.
  • Talk to the teacher before ordering anything
  • Set a budget, and decide new or second-hand
    Second-hand dresses are very common and keep their value. Many sell again later.
  • For a new made-to-order dress, allow several months
  • Try second-hand dresses on in person, or get a return window if buying online

Expect higher entry fees and longer days at higher levels.

Championships and majors

Budget for entry, travel, hotel, food, extra lessons and costume care.

Majors (major championships)

The big championships above a local feis, such as the Oireachtas, Nationals, the All-Irelands and Worlds. They run over several days and usually need a qualifying result. The teacher usually enters dancers for these, and travel is often involved.

Ways to save

  • Hold off on hard shoes, wigs and dresses until the teacher says. Lots of families buy too early. Second-hand is very common for all of these.
  • Second-hand dresses are very common and keep their value. Many sell again later.
  • A feis where solo dresses and fancy costumes aren't allowed, and dancers wear simple outfits, usually black. Cheaper and quicker to get ready for. The syllabus will say if it's one.
  • A small feis that only the host school's own dancers can enter. A friendly place for a first competition.
  • Register before the deadline: late entries often cost extra.

Quick answers

What are the biggest costs in Irish dance?

Class fees, shoes and feis entries are the regular costs. The solo dress is one of the biggest costs; rules govern when it's allowed, and championship dancers add travel to majors.

Should I buy a second-hand solo dress?

Second-hand dresses are very common and keep their value. Many sell again later.

When should we buy hard shoes, a wig or a dress?

Hold off on hard shoes, wigs and dresses until the teacher says. Lots of families buy too early. Second-hand is very common for all of these.
