WIDA rules in plain words

Short answers to the questions parents ask most about World Irish Dance Association (WIDA) rules. Taken from the WIDA competition rules.

  • Entering competitions
  • At the feis
  • Moving up
  • Premierships and championships
  • Shoes and costume
  • Changing schools

Entering competitions

Who can dance at a WIDA feis?

Anyone. WIDA feiseanna are open to dancers from every organization, and to independent dancers who aren't with a school.

Rulebook section 1.1

How do I enter, and can I get a refund?

All entries go through the Yafers website. Once paid, entries can't be refunded, even for illness or injury. You only get money back if the feis itself is cancelled.

Rulebook section 1.6–1.7

Which age group does my dancer dance in?

Their age on 1 January decides it for the whole year. A dancer who was 9 on 1 January dances Under 10 all year. Yafers works it out for you, and small age groups can be merged on the day.

Rulebook section 1.8

At the feis

Can I film or photograph my dancer?

Not while anyone is dancing. Photos and video are only allowed during award ceremonies, introductions and breaks. This protects children, and anyone filming will be asked to delete it and leave.

Rulebook section 2.3

Can I help or coach my dancer from the side of the stage?

No. Parents and teachers can't give directions while a dancer is competing, and only the dancers in that competition can wait at the side of the stage.

Rulebook section 2.5–2.6

Can I use hairspray in the hall?

No, because of allergies. Do hair before you go into the dancing hall. Smoking, vaping and alcohol aren't allowed in the hall either.

Rulebook section 2.1–2.2

What should my dancer know about lining up and awards?

Dancers go on stage in number order, and a dancer who arrives late may not be allowed to dance. To collect an award they must be in full costume, shoes included.

Rulebook section 3.3–3.4

What gets a dancer only 1 point for a round?

Starting early, stopping and not carrying on, bumping into someone on purpose, dancing a step on one foot but not the other, or a costume problem such as a wig or shoe falling off or a lace coming undone. Beginners get some leeway on starting and stopping.

Rulebook section 3.5–3.9, 16, 18.1

Who gets a medal or trophy?

Everyone in Jump 2-3 and the beginner reel is placed and gets a medal. Other solos award 1st to 3rd, and up to half the dancers when there are 10 or more. In premierships and championships everyone is placed, and 1st to 5th get trophies.

Rulebook section 3.2

Moving up

When does my dancer move up a grade?

When they win 1st place in a dance with 5 or more dancers, they must move up in that dance. With fewer than 5 dancers, the teacher can choose to move them up. It's one dance at a time.

Rulebook section 5.3–5.6

Can my dancer move back down a grade?

No. Once they move up, they stay up, and they must dance the new grade from their very next feis. If they're already entered at the lower grade, the teacher must tell that feis within 2 days. The only exception is a break of ten years or more.

Rulebook section 5.8

Can my dancer enter two grades at one feis?

Yes, they can also enter one grade up in the same dance and age group. For example, Beginner and Primary reel at the same feis.

Rulebook section 5.2

What about very young dancers?

Under-5 competitions have no grades, so nobody moves up from them. The Jump 2-3 competition is for the youngest dancers (up to Under 11 now, Under 7 only from 2027). Jump 2-3 dancers may also dance the Beginner reel, but nothing else.

Rulebook section 5.7, 3.1

Premierships and championships

What is a premiership, and which one does my dancer enter?

Two dances with the scores added together: reel and light jig at Beginner and Primary, reel and treble jig at Intermediate. Your dancer enters the premiership for the grade they're in for at least 2 of reel, light jig and treble jig, and only one per feis. They must also dance at least 2 solos that day. Winning one (with 5 or more dancers) moves both dances up.

Rulebook section 10–11

When does my dancer start Preliminary Championship?

After winning 4 dances at Intermediate, at least 2 of them hard shoe dances (light jig and single jig count too). Until then they stay in the Intermediate Premiership.

Rulebook section 11.8

When does my dancer move to Open Championship?

After winning 3 Preliminary Championships with at least 5 dancers (at least 3 in America and Australasia). Winning an Open Championship moves all their solo dances up to Open too, apart from hard shoe for young dancers who only danced soft shoe.

Rulebook section 12.2, 13.5

How does my dancer qualify for the World Championships?

The year before, they dance an Open solo championship or a premiership plus 2 solos at their own National Oireachtas. To dance an Open championship at an Oireachtas, they also need to have danced at a normal local feis in the 12 months before. Injured dancers can ask for an exemption once every 3 years.

Rulebook section 14–15

Shoes and costume

What shoes are allowed?

Soft shoes must be all black with black laces and no sparkle. Hard shoes must be all black with black laces (a white strap is fine), with no buckles or sparkle. A dancer who breaks the costume or shoe rules can't place in the top 3.

Rulebook section 18.2, 18.4

What are the rules for dresses?

No school names or personal names on any costume. Class dresses should be simpler than solo dresses, and Celtic designs are strongly recommended. Jewellery worn for religious reasons must be taped down.

Rulebook section 18.3, 18.5

What should my dancer wear between rounds?

Change in the changing rooms. If a dress is unzipped between rounds, wear a crop top or bodysuit underneath, keep arms in the sleeves and the front covered.

Rulebook section 2.7

Changing schools

What happens if we change schools?

Your dancer can keep competing straight away, with no ban. The new teacher tells the old teacher in writing. Your dancer can't use the old school's steps (except traditional sets), so they compete once they've learned the new school's steps.
