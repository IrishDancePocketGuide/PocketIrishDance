How Irish dance championships are judged

Grade competitions and championships are judged differently. That is why your dancer's round placings can look nothing like their overall result.

In grade competitions, each dance is its own competition. In a championship, a panel of judges decides one overall place. For each round, one judge from the panel is picked at random, and that judge's placings become everyone's placing for that round. So a round placing reflects only one judge, and can be very different from the overall place.

CLRG championships

Preliminary Championship

One competition of two rounds: soft shoe, then hard shoe. A panel of judges watches both rounds. For each round one judge is picked at random, and that judge's placings count as everyone's round placing. The overall place comes from all the judges' scores added up, so it can differ from the round placings.

Open Championship

Like Preliminary Championship but with three rounds: soft shoe, hard shoe, and a set round (own choice set). Each round is placed by one judge picked at random; the overall place comes from all the judges' scores added up. Judged by a panel (3, or 5 at majors). The path to Nationals, All-Irelands and Worlds.

The words you'll see on results

Grades (grade competitions)

All the levels below championship (Beginner 1, Beginner 2, Novice, Open Prizewinner). Each dance is its own competition, split by level and age. Where most dancers spend their first years. Not the same as grade exams.

Championship (Prelim, Open)

Prelim (PC) and Open (OC) competitions. PC is 2 rounds (soft shoe, hard shoe); OC adds a 3rd, the set round (own choice set). Judged by a panel, with one overall place. Unlike grades, where every dance is its own competition with its own award.

Light round (soft shoe round)

The soft shoe round of a championship: a reel or a slip jig. Usually the first round your dancer dances.

Heavy round (hard shoe round)

The hard shoe round of a championship: a treble jig or a hornpipe. Usually the second round.

Round placing

In a championship, where your dancer placed in one round. One judge from the panel is picked at random for each round, and that judge's placings are given to every dancer for that round. Round places can look very different from the overall place, because the other judges may have seen it differently.

Overall place

A dancer's final championship result, from every judge's scores for every round added together. It isn't worked out from the round placings. This is the place that counts for awards, moving up and qualifying.

Raw score (marks)

Points each judge gives. What the score tracker can log.

Irish points

Placings converted into points so different judges can be combined. Explains why the winner isn't always the dancer with the highest raw marks.

Recall

Being called back after the first rounds to dance the final round. A recall is itself an achievement worth celebrating.

Placement (podium)

Finishing high enough to be called up for an award. How many places are awarded depends on the size of the competition.

Adjudicator

A judge. In grades one judge places each dance. Championships use a panel: usually 3, 5 at major championships, and 6 at the All-Irelands with 5 counting.

Results posting

Results on paper boards at the venue, or published online afterwards. What parents photograph for the score tracker.

Why tracking each judge helps

Because each round placing comes from one judge, looking at the same judge over several feiseanna tells you much more than one result on its own.
